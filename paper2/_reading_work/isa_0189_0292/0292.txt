Abstract—Distributed drive electric vehicles are one of the
key focuses in the contemporary automotive industry, providing
greater flexibility for active safety control. The realization of
vehicle control relies on accurate vehicle driving state infor-
mation. Precise estimation of tire forces is essential, since tire
forces directly influence vehicle dynamics. This paper focuses on
distributed drive electric vehicles and proposes two estimation
methods: model-based method and machine learning method.
The model-based estimation method employs PID observers and
Unscented Kalman Filters to estimate longitudinal and lateral
tire forces respectively. The machine learning estimation method
utilizes Long Short-Term Memory networks for tire forces
estimation. The effectiveness and performance of two methods
are verified by MATLAB/CarSim. The results demonstrate that
the machine learning method provides more accurate tire force
estimation and exhibits robustness against measurement noise.
Index Terms —Distributed drive, State estimation, Machine
learning, LSTM
I. I NTRODUCTION
Distributed drive electric vehicles can fully take advantage
of independently driven motors, providing more flexibility for
vehicle dynamics control. Active safety technology, as part of
the development of intelligent automobiles, refers to safety
devices that prevent accidents, including steering systems,
well-performing chassis, power systems matched with the
chassis, and effective braking systems [1]. Both distributed
drive electric vehicles and active safety technology require
real-time and accurate control commands for the vehicle. How
to obtain vehicle state information during driving has attracted
much attention from researchers.
Some scholars [2] have tried to use special sensors, such
as optical sensors for detecting road surface, high-precision
Global Positioning System (GPS)/Inertial Measurement Unit
(IMU) combination modules, to achieve direct measurement
of vehicle driving state information (such as tire-road friction
coefficient TRFC, center of mass sideslip-angle, tire forces).
However, such sensors are costly and have demanding oper-
ating requirements, making them prone to large calibration
errors and temperature drift errors in complex driving sce-
narios. So they are only suitable for development and testing
stage, but currently cannot be applied to production vehicles.
Most vehicle driving states cannot be directly measured by on-
board sensors [3]. Automotive industry tends to use configured
sensors to obtain accessible driving information, estimate other
critical states, and then carry out subsequent control work.
II. R ELATED WORK
Model-based estimation methods can be broadly categorized
into two primary groups: Kalman filter algorithm, and estima-
tion algorithm based on observer design. Estimation algorithm
based on Kalman filter includes Kalman filter (KF), extended
Kalman filter (EKF) and unscented Kalman filter (UKF) [4],
[5], etc. KF is most commonly used algorithm, while EKF
and UKF are derived to solve nonlinear problems, providing
closer approximations to vehicle driving states. Estimation
algorithm based on observer can design nonlinear observer
through fuzzy, sliding mode or other methods, which introduce
feedback in observer to ensure algorithm convergence.
Laura [6]–[8] conducted some research on nonlinear state
estimation. Utilizing a nonlinear EKF algorithm, he estimated
tire forces without prior knowledge of tire characteristics or
external motion factors, which was applied to vehicle real-time
control. Ma [9] proposed a modular observer optimized by
genetic algorithm. He utilized a sliding mode observer and a
nonlinear full-dimensional observer separately to estimate tire
forces and vehicle states. Doumiati [10] et al. proposed an EKF
algorithm based on three different dynamic models to estimate
lateral load transfer and tire normal force. Real-vehicle tests
showed this method can reduce costs and provide real-time
state estimation. Jin [11] et al. proposed a new method
to estimate lateral tire forces using real-time measurement
information, which is based on an interacting multiple model
filter that integrates estimates from different models to improve
estimation accuracy.
Model-free state estimation utilizes machine learning (e.g.,
neural network NN, random forest RF) without relying on
physical models but requiring sufficient data. Pasterkamph and
Pacejka [12] used NNs to describe dynamic characteristics of

tires and suspensions, so as to estimate TRFC and sideslip-
angle. The optimal network was selected using a genetic
optimization algorithm from general network architecture.
Barbosa et al. [13] developed a tire lateral force predictor
using Gaussian process regression (GPR), which served as
a basis for tire dynamics analysis at large slip angles (up
to 8°). Xu et al. [14] predicted real-time tire force with an
intelligent tire system, incorporating three different machine
learning techniques. The sensor on the tire inner liner gener-
ated acceleration signals for training NNs to predict tire force.
Salari et al. [15] combined NNs and fuzzy logic to predict tire
normal force. NN blocks with different dynamics estimated
mass distribution, then the fuzzy classification logic deter-
mined which block to activated to further estimate dynamic
normal force. Wang et al. [16] introduced an adaptive tire
force calculation method based on an improved Levenberg-
Marquarelt multi-module self-organizing feedforward neural
network, which reduced the system computational complexity
and storage space. Additionally, it employed EKF and moving
average further improved estimation accuracy.
Model-based estimation methods typically rely on inter-
pretable mathematical models. In contrast, machine learning
estimation methods do not need to use complex model in-
formation in estimator, and avoid errors induced by model
mismatch and parameter uncertainty. By training NNs with
time series data, the temporal structure hidden in data can be
accurately captured. Consequently, machine learning methods
have obvious advantages when dealing with nonlinear tire
dynamics problems.
This paper designs two methods, model-based and machine
learning approach, to estimate tire force, and compares both
methods using MATLAB/CarSim simulation platform. The
rest of this article is organized as follows: Section III in-
troduces specific models used for tire force estimation and
a model-based method. Section IV introduces and designs a
machine learning method. Section V gives results and data
analysis of simulation. Finally, Section VI gives conclusions.

suitable for processing and predicting time series. Compared
with traditional RNN structure, LSTM can avoid gradient
vanishing or exploding problem.

and three gate structures, namely input gate, output gate, and
forget gate. The three different gate structures depend on the
state of previous moment and the input of current moment.

Main structure of LSTM network is shown in Fig. 3. LSTM

This paper uses two different multi-input multi-output
LSTM structures to estimate Fxij and Fyij in turn. First, Fxij
are estimated based on Tij, ωij, ax, ay, r, δ, and then Fyij are
estimated based on ax, ay, r, δ, ˆFxij.
The training parameters of LSTM network include but are
not limited to the following: The gradient estimation correction
method employs Adaptive Moment Estimation Algorithm. As
the most commonly used adjustment method, it combines
the advantages of RMSprop method and momentum method,
enabling simultaneous adjustment of the learning rate and
gradient. The mini-batch size refers to dividing total samples
into several batches, performing an iteration every time a batch
is encountered, and calculating average loss function from
batch level, to improve calculation efficiency. Max epochs
should be set to the number of epochs when the network loss
function converges to the minimum value to avoid overfitting
caused by too many epochs. The gradient threshold is to
prevent gradient explosion. If the initial learning rate is too
large, it may fail to find the global optimal value and diverge;
if too small, it may settle into a local optimum. The strategy
for adjusting the learning rate involves starting with a larger
learning rate to speed up convergence, and then gradually
reducing it as the training process deepens to complete detailed
learning. This adjustment process requires determining the
number of epochs at which learning rate begins to decrease
and the proportional factor for each decrease. Reference [20]
here uses random grid search method to divide parameters
into several groups within a certain range, which randomly
select different groups of parameters for training. Parameters
corresponding to the best training effect are chosen. According
to training results and computing power of hardware, Table I

After the NN model is established in the MATLAB 2023
Deep Learning Toolbox, it is necessary to use a diverse
dataset for training, including as many driving conditions as
possible. This paper utilizes CarSim simulation software to
collect training data. Here, training set is obtained on the
Handling Course track, with a total distance of approximately
2.27km. It includes straight roads and various combined curve

acceleration and braking. The track is comprehensive and
complex, mirroring actual road conditions, and has abundant
vehicle dynamic state information.

VI. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, two methods are proposed: model-based and
LSTM. The performance of these two methods in tire force
estimation is compared using MATLAB and CarSim. Both
methods can successfully estimate the tire longitudinal and
lateral forces in a distributed drive electric vehicle. LSTM
method can provide better estimates of tire forces. When
adding Gaussian white noise of same intensity, the estimation
results of LSTM method are more noise robust than those of
the other method. This study shows that LSTM method based
on machine learning is feasible for tire force estimation. Future
research will focus on reducing estimation error and improving
adaptability of method in extreme conditions.
