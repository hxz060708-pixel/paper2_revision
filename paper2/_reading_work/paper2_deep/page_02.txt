reported, including resilient adaptive intermittent control under sensor deception attacks [20] and K-filter-based quantized
control under joint deception attacks [21]. Nevertheless, maintaining prescribedH∞ performance for IT-2 fuzzy CPSs under
randomly occurring deception attacks remains a challenging issue.
Communication efficiency is another key bottleneck in practical IT-2 fuzzy CPSs. Event-triggered mechanisms (ETMs)
[22,23]determinedatatransmissionaccordingtoapredefinedtriggeringcriterion, therebyreducingredundantcommunication
and bandwidth consumption. However, conventional static ETMs use fixed thresholds and cannot adapt well to state
fluctuations caused by attacks, disturbances, or actuator anomalies. Adaptive event-triggered mechanisms (AETMs) [24, 25]
and dynamic triggering strategies [26] adjust triggering parameters according to real-time or historical state information.
For IT-2 fuzzy CPSs, a suitable AETM should reduce redundant transmissions while remaining compatible with fuzzy
membership information and resilient control requirements.
In addition to cyber-layer risks and communication constraints, physical-layer reliability is also critical for CPSs. Ac-
tuators may suffer from hardware aging, mechanical wear, or harsh operating environments, which can cause stochastic
degradation of the control channel [27, 28]. Markov processes are commonly used to describe switching among different
actuator failure modes [29, 30]. If such failures are not considered in controller design, the resulting control input may be
weakened or distorted. When actuator failures occur together with deception attacks, the transmitted state and the effec-
tive control input are both corrupted. Hence, a secure control strategy for IT-2 fuzzy CPSs should simultaneously handle
cyber-layer data falsification, physical-layer actuator uncertainty, and limited communication resources.
Lyapunov–Krasovskii functional (LKF) techniques provide an effective framework for stability andH∞ performance
analysis in networked and sampled-data systems. To reduce conservatism, advanced LKF and looped-functional techniques
have been developed to exploit sampling intervals and state information. For instance, a state-dependent looped Lyapunov
functional was designed in [31] to incorporate inter-sample dynamics. An augmented functional with delay-interaction
and quadratic-time looped terms was proposed in [32] to exploit delay cross-information and integral states. A boundary-
information-based looped Lyapunov functional combined with integral inequalities was developed in [33] to address sampled-
datasynchronizationofuncertainstochasticneuralnetworks. Despitetheseadvances, adaptiveevent-triggeredH ∞ controlfor
IT-2 fuzzy CPSs affected by stochastic deception attacks and Markov-type actuator failures remains insufficiently explored.
Building on the above discussion, this study addresses adaptive event-triggeredH∞ control for IT-2 fuzzy CPSs affected
by stochastic deception attacks and Markov-type actuator failures. The principal contributions are fourfold:
1) A unified adaptive event-triggered closed-loop model is established for IT-2 fuzzy CPSs under concurrent cyber attacks
and actuator failures.
2) An adaptive triggering scheme is proposed, with its threshold updated according to the state error. By adjusting the
threshold online according to real-time state-error information, the proposed mechanism reduces redundant transmis-
sions while maintaining the desired control performance.
3) A region-dependent fuzzy control scheme is developed by exploiting the sign information of the membership-function
derivatives. The resulting region partition enables the control and triggering parameters to be jointly designed for each
operational region.
4) A two-sided FMF-dependent looped functional is formulated to incorporate state information from both subintervals
of each sampling period. This construction reduces conservatism and yields tractable LMI-basedH∞ stability and
synthesis conditions.
