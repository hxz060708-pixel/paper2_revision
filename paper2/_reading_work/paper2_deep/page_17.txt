5. Conclusion
This study has addressed adaptive event-triggeredH∞ control of IT-2 fuzzy CPSs affected simultaneously by stochastic
deception attacks and Markov-type actuator failures. An AETM has been employed to limit unnecessary data transmissions
by updating the triggering threshold online using real-time state-error information. In addition, a region-dependent piecewise
fuzzy control strategy has been developed by incorporating derivative information from the membership functions. Based
on an FMF-dependent and time-dependent looped functional, stability criteria have been obtained to guarantee closed-loop
asymptotic stability while achieving a prescribedH ∞ disturbance attenuation level. The controller gains and adaptive
event-triggering weight matrices have been jointly determined through tractable LMIs, while a bounding technique has been
employed to address the norm-bounded uncertainty induced by Markov-type actuator failures.
An engineering-motivated PMSM example and a numerical IT-2 fuzzy example have demonstrated the effectiveness and
applicability of the developed method. Further research will extend the current IT-2 fuzzy control methodology to observer-
based output feedback control, since not all system states are measurable in practical industrial applications. Secure control
under more sophisticated cyberattack scenarios, such as DoS attacks, and practical network constraints, such as partially
unknown Markov transition probabilities, also deserve further investigation.
