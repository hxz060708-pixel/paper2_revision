Adaptive Event-Triggered Control for Interval Type-2 Fuzzy Cyber-Physical Systems
Under Deception Attacks and Actuator Failures: A
Membership-Function-Derivative-Dependent Switching Perspective
Xiaoqing Lia,∗, Haoxiang Zhaoa, Liang Hana, Jun Chengb, Kaibo Shic
aSchool of Electrical Engineering and Automation, Hefei University of Technology, Hefei 230009, China
bCollege of Mathematics and Statistics, Guangxi Normal University, Guilin 541006, China
cSchool of Electronic Information and Electrical Engineering, Chengdu University, Chengdu 610106, China
Abstract
This article addresses adaptive event-triggered control for interval type-2 (IT-2) fuzzy cyber-physical systems (CPSs) affected
by stochastic deception attacks and Markov-type actuator failures. Since nonlinear CPSs face cyber-layer attacks as well
as physical-layer component failures, while their communication resources are often limited, an adaptive event-triggered
mechanism (AETM) is employed to reduce redundant network transmissions through dynamic adjustment of the trigger-
ing threshold based on real-time state-error information. Specifically, stochastic deception attacks are characterized by a
Bernoulli process, and actuator failures are modeled by Markov switching fault matrices. Accordingly, a unified closed-loop
model is established. Then, a looped functional dependent on fuzzy membership functions (FMFs) is constructed to exploit
membership-function information and sampled-data dynamics. Based on this functional, stability criteria are obtained to
guarantee asymptotic stability of the closed-loop system while achieving a prescribedH∞ disturbance attenuation level.
Moreover, joint design of the controller gains and adaptive triggering parameters is formulated through tractable linear
matrix inequalities (LMIs). Validation is finally carried out with an engineering-motivated permanent magnet synchronous
motor example and a numerical IT-2 fuzzy system to demonstrate the effectiveness and feasibility of the developed method.
Keywords:Interval type-2 (IT-2) fuzzy cyber-physical systems (CPSs), Adaptive event-triggered mechanism (AETM),
Deception attacks, Markov-type actuator failures
1. Introduction
Driven by advances in communication, sensing, and computing, cyber-physical systems (CPSs) have attracted growing
attention in modern control research. Applications of CPSs span intelligent transportation, smart grids, and advanced
aviation [1–3]. By integrating cyber and physical components, CPSs enable real-time monitoring and coordinated control.
However, practical physical plants usually involve nonlinear dynamics, parameter perturbations, and external disturbances
[4, 5]. Therefore, developing control methods that can handle nonlinearities, uncertainties, and network-induced constraints
is crucial to achieving reliable and stable operation of CPSs [6].
To represent nonlinear dynamics in a tractable form, the Takagi–Sugeno (T–S) fuzzy approach [7, 8] is employed for
nonlinear system analysis and control design. Through membership-function-based weighting of multiple local linear subsys-
tems, T–S fuzzy models can effectively capture the behavior of complex nonlinear systems. Nevertheless, classical T–S fuzzy
models usually depend on precise membership grades, which may be sensitive to parameter perturbations and measurement
uncertainty. IT-2 fuzzy systems [9, 10] address this limitation by introducing upper and lower membership functions to de-
scribe the footprint of uncertainty. Recent studies have applied IT-2 fuzzy methods to non-uniform sampling [11], vehicular
communication [12], and multi-agentH ∞ consensus [13]. These results confirm the usefulness of IT-2 fuzzy models, but
secure and reliable control of IT-2 fuzzy CPSs under coupled cyber and physical anomalies remains insufficiently developed.
WhenIT-2fuzzysystemsareimplementedovercommunicationnetworks, theirflexibilityisimproved, butseveralnetwork-
induced constraints are also introduced. Transmission delays, packet dropouts, and bandwidth limitations may degrade the
closed-loop performance [14, 15]. Moreover, the openness of network channels exposes CPSs to malicious cyberattacks.
Deception attacks [16, 17] and denial-of-service (DoS) attacks [18, 19] are two common cyber threats to CPSs.. Unlike DoS
attacks, which block communication channels and cause packet losses, deception attacks inject falsified data into transmitted
signals. This stealthy modification can seriously degrade closed-loop performance. Several secure control methods have been

reported, including resilient adaptive intermittent control under sensor deception attacks [20] and K-filter-based quantized
control under joint deception attacks [21]. Nevertheless, maintaining prescribedH∞ performance for IT-2 fuzzy CPSs under
randomly occurring deception attacks remains a challenging issue.
Communication efficiency is another key bottleneck in practical IT-2 fuzzy CPSs. Event-triggered mechanisms (ETMs)
[22,23]determinedatatransmissionaccordingtoapredefinedtriggeringcriterion, therebyreducingredundantcommunication
and bandwidth consumption. However, conventional static ETMs use fixed thresholds and cannot adapt well to state
fluctuations caused by attacks, disturbances, or actuator anomalies. Adaptive event-triggered mechanisms (AETMs) [24, 25]
and dynamic triggering strategies [26] adjust triggering parameters according to real-time or historical state information.
For IT-2 fuzzy CPSs, a suitable AETM should reduce redundant transmissions while remaining compatible with fuzzy
membership information and resilient control requirements.
In addition to cyber-layer risks and communication constraints, physical-layer reliability is also critical for CPSs. Ac-
tuators may suffer from hardware aging, mechanical wear, or harsh operating environments, which can cause stochastic
degradation of the control channel [27, 28]. Markov processes are commonly used to describe switching among different
actuator failure modes [29, 30]. If such failures are not considered in controller design, the resulting control input may be
weakened or distorted. When actuator failures occur together with deception attacks, the transmitted state and the effec-
tive control input are both corrupted. Hence, a secure control strategy for IT-2 fuzzy CPSs should simultaneously handle
cyber-layer data falsification, physical-layer actuator uncertainty, and limited communication resources.
Lyapunov–Krasovskii functional (LKF) techniques provide an effective framework for stability andH∞ performance
analysis in networked and sampled-data systems. To reduce conservatism, advanced LKF and looped-functional techniques
have been developed to exploit sampling intervals and state information. For instance, a state-dependent looped Lyapunov
functional was designed in [31] to incorporate inter-sample dynamics. An augmented functional with delay-interaction
and quadratic-time looped terms was proposed in [32] to exploit delay cross-information and integral states. A boundary-
information-based looped Lyapunov functional combined with integral inequalities was developed in [33] to address sampled-
datasynchronizationofuncertainstochasticneuralnetworks. Despitetheseadvances, adaptiveevent-triggeredH ∞ controlfor
IT-2 fuzzy CPSs affected by stochastic deception attacks and Markov-type actuator failures remains insufficiently explored.
Building on the above discussion, this study addresses adaptive event-triggeredH∞ control for IT-2 fuzzy CPSs affected
by stochastic deception attacks and Markov-type actuator failures. The principal contributions are fourfold:
1) A unified adaptive event-triggered closed-loop model is established for IT-2 fuzzy CPSs under concurrent cyber attacks
and actuator failures.
2) An adaptive triggering scheme is proposed, with its threshold updated according to the state error. By adjusting the
threshold online according to real-time state-error information, the proposed mechanism reduces redundant transmis-
sions while maintaining the desired control performance.
3) A region-dependent fuzzy control scheme is developed by exploiting the sign information of the membership-function
derivatives. The resulting region partition enables the control and triggering parameters to be jointly designed for each
operational region.
4) A two-sided FMF-dependent looped functional is formulated to incorporate state information from both subintervals
of each sampling period. This construction reduces conservatism and yields tractable LMI-basedH∞ stability and
synthesis conditions.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the system model and formulates the control problem. Section
3 develops the stability analysis and controller design. Section 4 validates the proposed approach through an engineering-
motivated example and a numerical example. Section 5 concludes the paper and outlines potential directions for future
research.

2.2. Adaptive Event-Triggered Mechanism

waste of limited communication bandwidth. To alleviate the communication burden while preserving the desired control

2.3. Characterization of Deception Attacks
In NCSs, transmitted signals may be corrupted due to malicious interference in communication channels. In particular,
deception attacks can inject falsified data into the transmission process, which may distort the original information and
severely degrade system performance.
To characterize the occurrence of deception attacks, letθ(t)denote a Bernoulli random variable taking values in{0,1},
whereθ(t) = 1indicates that the transmitted data is compromised by the attacker, andθ(t) = 0otherwise. Such a Bernoulli
process is commonly employed to describe randomly occurring deception attacks in networked control systems. It is assumed
thatθ(t)satisfies the following statistical property:
E{θ(t)}= ¯θ (7)
where ¯θ∈[0,1]represents the probability of a deception attack. The stochastic processθ(t)is assumed to be independent
of the system state, actuator failure mode, and external disturbance.
Consequently, under the considered attack model, the signalxc(t)received by the controller is expressed as:

Remark 2.3.Different from Denial-of-Service attacks that aim to block communication channels and cause packet dropouts,
deception attacks maliciously tamper with the transmitted data packets to degrade system performance or even destabilize
the system. The expression (8) unifies normal data transmission (θ(t) = 0) and successful deception attacks (θ(t) = 1)

limitations on the attack intensity, since adversaries cannot inject unbounded signals into the communication network.

5. Conclusion
This study has addressed adaptive event-triggeredH∞ control of IT-2 fuzzy CPSs affected simultaneously by stochastic
deception attacks and Markov-type actuator failures. An AETM has been employed to limit unnecessary data transmissions
by updating the triggering threshold online using real-time state-error information. In addition, a region-dependent piecewise
fuzzy control strategy has been developed by incorporating derivative information from the membership functions. Based
on an FMF-dependent and time-dependent looped functional, stability criteria have been obtained to guarantee closed-loop
asymptotic stability while achieving a prescribedH ∞ disturbance attenuation level. The controller gains and adaptive
event-triggering weight matrices have been jointly determined through tractable LMIs, while a bounding technique has been
employed to address the norm-bounded uncertainty induced by Markov-type actuator failures.
An engineering-motivated PMSM example and a numerical IT-2 fuzzy example have demonstrated the effectiveness and
applicability of the developed method. Further research will extend the current IT-2 fuzzy control methodology to observer-
based output feedback control, since not all system states are measurable in practical industrial applications. Secure control
under more sophisticated cyberattack scenarios, such as DoS attacks, and practical network constraints, such as partially
unknown Markov transition probabilities, also deserve further investigation.
