Comment on JSCE-24-0030 
March 1, 2024 
 
This article has addressed the problem of how to implement dynamic output 
feedback asynchronous control in discrete time-delayed neural networks. This control 
method takes into account the limitations of network communication, where only a 
limited number of sensors and actuators can exchange information with the controller 
through a shared communication network at each sampling period. The randomness of 
this communication is described by a Markov process, meaning that the change in 
network state between different sampling periods is random and can affect the control 
performance of the network. 
Additionally, to handle the possibility of data packet losses during 
communication, a Bernoulli stochastic process is utilized to describe this behavior. 
This implies that during the information transmission process, data packets may be 
lost with a certain probability, posing a challenge to the stability and control 
effectiveness of the neural network. 
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a method of transforming the 
neural network into a Markovian jumping system and designing a dynamic output 
feedback asynchronous controller based on this transformation. The core idea of this 
approach is to derive sufficient conditions for exponentially bounded control of the 
neural network using the theories of Lyapunov stability and Markovian jumping 
systems. This ensures the asymptotic exponential boundedness stability of the neural 
network even in the presence of randomness and packet losses in network 
communication. 
By employing this dynamic output feedback asynchronous control strategy, it is 
possible to effectively enhance the control performance and stability of neural 
networks in complex communication environments, providing new solutions for 
neural network control problems in practical applications. 
This article is clearly written and easy to understand, mathematically correct and 
strict. I think the results of this paper will be noticed by other scholars in control 
theory. Therefore, I am happy to recommend this paper for publication in your 
journal, subject to the minor revision as suggested below. 
Some suggestion on corrections: 
1. P.4 Line 39: Considering the abbreviation LMIs are used later, the first time you 
use LMI, the author should give the full name to avoid ambiguity; 
2. Why should we study this problem, what is the actual motivation for this article 
and the research background of this paper is not clear. More recent literature may 
be updated to enhance the research motivation of this article; 
3. P.6: Hope that the author can use curly braces in the Eq (5) and Eq (7) to make 
these equations be more readable. 
Overall, if the author can take the above problem seriously, this paper can be 
accepted after some minor modifications.
