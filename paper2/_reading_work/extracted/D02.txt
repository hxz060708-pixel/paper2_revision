Chaoqun Zhu*, Hui Xing, and Pan Zhang 
C. Zhu, H. Xing  and P. Zhang are with the College of Electrical and Information Engineering, Lanzhou University of

Abstract: This paper investigates the asynchronous control problem for a class of discrete -time-delayed neural 
networks with medium access constraints and packet dropouts. To prevent the transmission of data from col lisions, 
only a limited number of sensors and actuators can exchange information with the controller through the shared 
communication network at each sampling period, which is characterized by Markov stochastic processes. In 
addition, Bernoulli stochastic processes are utilized to describe the possibility of packet -loss behaviors in the 
process of information transmission. In the context of asymptotic exponential boundedn ess stability, a dynamic 
output feedback asynchronous approach is proposed by transform ing neural net works into a type of Markovian 
jumping system. In such a framework, sufficient conditions are derived for the exponentially bounded control of 
neural networks in terms of theories in Lyapunov stability and Markovian jumping systems. Specifically, Different 
from existing results based on the assumption of the full -rank control matrix, a more general method of matrix 
decoupling is developed by utilizing singul ar value decomposition technology in the case of the closed -loop 
system’s control matrix being non-full column rank. In the meanwhile, the desired controller parameters are solved 
by the proposed method of singular value decomposition to guarantee the ultimate boundedness of the closed -loop 
system in the mean square. Finally, an illustrative example is provided to demonstrate the effectiveness of the 
developed control algorithm. 
 
Keywords: Neural networks, asynchronous control, dynamic output feedback, medi um access constraints, packet

1. INTRODUCTION 
 
Networked control system (NCS) is a closed -loop system that connects sensors, actuators, and controllers distributed 
in different locations through a shared network connection [1,2]. Compared to the traditional point -to-point control 
system, the networked control system has structural characteristics such as modular design, good expansibility, and 
flexibility. Among them, neural network systems (NNSs) are also a type of networked control system. From the 
perspective of information processin g, the NNSs is a nonlinear system that mimics the way signals are transmitted 
between biological neural structures and carries out information processing and operations. The NNSs has good 
scalability and convenience, as well as strong information synthesis ability, enabling the realization of high -speed 
control algorithms for complex large -scale network systems. The application of NNSs has permeated various fields, 
including pattern recognition, signal processing, image encryption, and multi -agent control [3-6]. The form of network 
communication is relied upon by each node of the neural network for data transmission, leading to the inevitable 
emergence of certain communication constraints. These include networked time -delays [7,8], medium access 
constraints [9,10], packet dropouts [11,12], limits on data rates and quantization effects [13,14]. The presence of 
communication constraints often leads to the deterioration of system performance and even jeopardizes system stability. 
Fundamentally, most of the commu nication constraints mentioned above are caused by the limited data transmission 
rate and insufficient capacity of the communication medium of NNSs. 
The transmission of information is limited by the bandwidth of the communication network, and factors such as node 
competition and packet collision often occur during data transmission in the channel. For NNSs that contain a large 
number of network nodes (such as sensors, actuators, or subsystems). Inevitably, the system will suffer from access 
constraints and packet dropouts. Medium access constraints and packet dropouts can result in incomplete transmission 
of system data, which can impact system performance and even lead to system instability. Therefore, it is necessary to 
adopt effective strategies to address the negative effects of medium access constraints and packet dropouts on the 
system. The application of the communication scheduling protocols is the primary idea for addressing the impact of 
medium access constraints on the system. The prevailing commun ication scheduling protocols mainly include the 
round-robin (RR) protocol [15 -19], the weighted try -once-discard (WTOD) protocol [20 -23], and the stochastic 
communication protocol (SCP) [24 -26]. The impact of packet dropouts on the system is usually addressed by modeling 
it using either the Bernoulli independent identity distribution or the Markov stochastic distribution. The research on 
medium access constraints of NNSs has currently yielded corresponding results. The authors of literature [16] 
investigate the cooperative control problem of a class of distributed NNSs with time -varying delay under the RR 
scheduling protocol. To name a few, The authors of literature [17] investigate the output feedback control problem of a

the authors designed a resilient set membership estimator for a class of discrete -time amnesia neural networks with 
mixed delays and WTOD protocol to achieve efficient es timation of states. Due to medium access constraints, real 
networks have complex and random communication characteristics. Therefore, the RR protocol and the WTOD 
protocol are unable to satisfy the requirements of neural networks with such random communication characteristics. For 
the problem of stochastic characterization of medium access constraints, NNSs has been further studied by researchers, 
and some relevant research has bee n conducted. The authors of literatu re [25] investigated the finite time hor izon H∞

protocol is utilized  to alleviate of data blockage in the communication networks. Current research on the impact of 
packet dropouts on the system is also drawing widespread attention [27 -29]. The authors of literature [27] discuss the 
problem of state estimation for NNSs with distributed time delays and data packet dropouts. The authors of the 
literature [28] studied the problem of asynchronous filtering of neural networks with variable packet dropouts  rates. 
Most of the current research  on neural network systems only considers a single medium access constraint or packet 
dropouts problems, and very few consider them simultaneously. Meanwhile, most researchers have either considered 
the media access constraints for sensor -to-actuator channel or actuator -to-controller channel when investigating the 
neural network systems, while few studies have investigated the problem of simultaneous medium access constraints on 
both the sensor-to-actuator channel and the actuator-to-controller channel. However, in real-world network applications, 
the media access constraints and packet dropouts often exist simultaneously. More significantly, the media access 
restrictions and packet dropouts often exist in both sensor -to-controller channels and controller -to-actuator channels. 
Therefore, the simultaneous medium access constraints and packet dropouts problems in the sensor -to-controller 
channel and controller-to-actuator channel discussed in this paper are more challenging and practical. 
To address the issue o f unpredictable structural changes caused by networks. The common solution is to transform 
the NCS with medium access constraints into a switching system, where mode switching is managed by the 
communication protocol. Based on this, the problem has gained some relevant research [9,30,31]. However, most of the 
current analytical methods for switching systems are overly idealized , and the model switching of the controller tends to 
be synchronized with model switching of the system. In other words, the network control system has been able to obtain 
in advance information about the operating modes in which the system is going to switch. From the perspective of 
control practice, this assumption is unrealistic for the controller to keep synchronous with the switching mode of the 
system due to the fact that the mode switching of system is determined by medium access constraints and packet 
dropouts in a stochastic fashion. This motivates us to develop current investigation for the asynchronous control of 
neural netwo rks subject to medium access constraints and packet dropouts. In particular, network -induced 
communication constraint problem leads to a strong coupling between the controller parameters and the communication 
parameters, which poses a great challenge in solving the controller parameters. Fortunately, The Singular Value 
Decomposition (SVD) technique can effectively address the aforementioned issues of strong coupling [32 -34]. In the 
literature [32], the authors discuss the issues of finite-time stability and finite-time boundedness in networked predictive 
control systems. The SVD method is used to address issues related to time delays and packet dropouts, while a 
predictive control strategy is proposed to guarantee system stability. The authors of the literature [34] study a class of 
asynchronous H ∞ control problems with random packet dropouts and time -delayed systems, where the SVD technique 
is used to decouple the coupling terms when solving the LMI. Nevertheless, all of the above literature is based on the 
use of the SVD technique to deal with st rongly coupled terms under the condition of full column full rank of the control 
matrix. But the influence of medium access constraints and control practice constraints, the control matrix of system are 
often not full-column rank. In such a circumstance, the traditional SVD technology clearly becomes infeasible and there 
is a definite need for developing a novel SVD method to obtain the appropriate control parameters in the case of control 
matrix being non-full column rank, which is another motivation of current investigation. 
To give the response to the above statement, the asynchronous control issue based on the dynamic output feedback 
strategy is investigated for NNSs with medium access constraints and stochastic packet dropouts in this paper. 
Especially, for the case that stochastic access constraints cause the control matrix of system to be non -full column rank, 
a novel matrix decoupling method is proposed, and satisfactory controller gains are obtained by using developed SVD 
method and LMIs techniques. Following the summary of the main contributions of this paper over previous literature: 1) 
By mapping method, neural networks subject to stochastic access constraints and packet dropouts are transformed into 
Markov jump systems with multiple modes, which simplifies the difficulty of stability analysis. 2) Based on the 
established augmented model of neural networks, sufficient conditions are derived for the exponential boundedness of 
the system in terms of the asynchronous dynamic output feedback strategy. 3 ) By employing the proposed novel SVD 
method, the control parameters are solved in the case of control matrix being a non -full column rank to guarantee the 
ultimate boundedness of the closed-loop system in the mean square sense. 
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, a class of discrete -time delayed neural networks with 
medium access constraints and packet dropouts is introduced and the dynamic output feedback (DOF) asynchronous 
controller is proposed. In Section 3, the ultimate bound edness control issue of the closed -loop system is investigated 
based on the Lyapunov stability theory and LMIs technology. Furthermore, the desired controller parameters are 
obtained by the proposed SVD method. An illustrative example is provided in Section 4 to demonstrate the 
effectiveness of the proposed results. Finally, conclusions are drawn in Section 5.

5. CONCLUSION 
 
The asynchronous DOF control problem has been investigated for a class of discrete -time delayed neural networks 
subject to medium access constraints and stochastic packet dropouts in this paper. Due to the presence of a limited 
bandwidth network, it  is assumed that only a part of the sensors and actuators can gain network medium to 
communicate with the controller in each sampling period, and the selected sensors and actuators ar e determined by 
stochastic access protocols. Another fundamental issue is the consideration of stochastic packet dropout that follows 
Bernoulli independent identically distributed process during the data transmission. In such a context, sufficient 
conditions are derived for the exponentially ultimately bounded of the dynamics o f the closed-loop system in the mean 
square sense by utilizing an asynchronous DOF controller. Furthermore, a more general method of matrix decoupling is 
developed by utilizing SVD te chnology in the case of control matrices being non -full column rank, and the control 
parameters with desired performance are solved by the presented method of singular value decomposition. Finally, an 
illustrative example is provided to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed control method.
