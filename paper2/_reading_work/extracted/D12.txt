and g of the functional equation f 2 + g2 = 1 are expressed as f = sin h and g =
cos h respectively, where h is an entire function. In Gross [2] also proved that the
meromorphic solutions f and g of the functional equation f 3 + g3 = 1 are expressed

The present authors consider the the precise expressions of the entire solutions of
the following two Fermat-type ordinary diﬀerential equations:
(a0f + a1f ′)2 + (a0f + a2f ′)2 = p (1)
and
(a0f + a1f ′)2 + (a0f + a2f ′)2 = eg, (2)
where a0, a 1 and a2 are complex numbers with a0 ̸= 0 and |a1| + |a2| ̸= 0, while p and
g are polynomials.
Conclusion: By using classical Nevanlinna’s theory and results obtained in [1, 2,
3]), the present authors mainly study the existence questions and the precise expressions
of entire solutions of the Fermat-type ordinary diﬀerential equations (1) and (2). The
results of the present paper are very interesting. The analysis and the reasoning of the
present paper is new and deep. The results obtained in the present paper are right.
The use of English language is sometimes a bit clumsy, and the Fermat-type ordinary

1. Page 1. Since the present authors use the classical Nevanlinna theory to prove the
main result. There fore, I suggest the present authors add the following sentences at
the beginning of the “Introduction”:
Throughout this paper, by meromorphic functions we will always mean meromor-
phic functions in the complex plane. We adopt the standard notations in the Nevan-
linna theory of meromorphic functions as explained in W.K.Hayman[5], I.Laine [6] and
L.Yang [7]. It will be convenient to let E denote any set of positive real numbers of
ﬁnite linear measure, not necessarily the same at each occurrence. For a non-constant
meromorphic function h, we denote by T (r, h) the Nevanlinna characteristic of h and
by S(r, h) any quantity satisfying S(r, h) = o(T (r, h)), as r → ∞ and r ̸∈ E.
